Lesson 1 · The basics

What forex is—and how to read a pair

Lesson 1 of 4

Forex (foreign exchange) is a market where one currency is exchanged for another. A pair compares the value of its first currency with its second.

Example: EUR/USD
EUR is the base currency; USD is the quote currency. A price of 1.0800 means one euro is quoted at 1.08 US dollars. It is an illustration, not a live quote.

A pair’s price can move up or down. Knowing the pair’s direction does not tell you whether a trade is suitable or profitable.

Lesson 2 · Price and costs

Pips and the spread

Lesson 2 of 4

A pip is a commonly used unit for describing a currency pair’s price movement. For many pairs it is 0.0001; for many yen pairs it is 0.01. Some platforms display an extra fractional pip.

The spread
The spread is the difference between a market’s buy (ask) and sell (bid) prices. It is one of the costs to understand before trading, and it can vary with the pair and market conditions.

Actual pricing and costs depend on the provider and account terms. Check those details rather than assuming every pair or platform is the same.

Lesson 3 · Leverage and risk

Leverage can magnify outcomes

Lesson 3 of 4

Leverage lets a trader control a larger market exposure than the funds set aside as margin. Margin is collateral required to maintain that exposure; it is not a cap on possible loss.

Keep in mind
Leverage can magnify gains and losses. A relatively small market move can have a large effect on account equity, and losses can exceed expectations. Rules and protections vary by provider and location.

Understand the provider’s leverage, margin and close-out terms before using any leveraged product. Practising on this site does not remove real-world risk.

Lesson 4 · Before you practise

Plan first; protect your limits

Lesson 4 of 4

A trading plan sets out the conditions you would use to make and review a decision. It can include what you are learning, how you would enter and exit a practice trade, and what would make you stand aside.

Before a practice trade
Know the possible loss, choose a position size that fits your own limits, and decide how you will respond if the idea is wrong. Stop orders may not fill at the requested price in fast or gapping markets.

There is no universally safe risk amount. Never trade money you cannot afford to lose, and do not treat this learning material as personal financial advice.

Intermediate: market structure, trends and risk
  • Market structure: swing highs and lows can help describe an uptrend, downtrend or range; they do not guarantee what happens next.
  • Support and resistance: areas where buying or selling interest has previously slowed a move. These are zones of interest, not guaranteed turning points.
  • Trend analysis: describe a market as generally rising, falling or moving sideways, while keeping the chosen timeframe in view.
  • Risk management: understand possible loss, position size, stop orders and leverage before practice; no tool prevents all losses.
Advanced: Smart Money Concepts (SMC)
  • Liquidity: traders may study areas where orders could be concentrated, such as prior highs and lows; this is an analytical idea, not a promise of price behaviour.
  • Break of Structure (BOS): a break of a swing level in the direction of a described structure, which may be interpreted as continuation evidence.
  • Change of Character (CHoCH): a break against a prior direction that some traders watch for a possible shift; it does not confirm a reversal.
  • Fair Value Gap (FVG): a three-candle pattern some traders interpret as an imbalance and study as a possible area of interest.
Psychology and planning
  • Discipline: practise following a written process rather than chasing a move or reacting emotionally to a loss.
  • Trading plan: write down the conditions, review process and risk limits you intend to follow. A plan cannot guarantee a result.
